# Research memo template

Use this shape for the deliverable. Headings can be renamed; the order matters because the reader may stop after section 1.

```
# [Question as a title]
## A research memo based on [primary texts]

Prepared [date]. Sources read in full: [...]. Locator conventions: [print pages from the PDF / from the book's subject index because only an EPUB was supplied / chapter and section for texts without pagination]. Web-sourced material in sections [n] carries verification labels ([F]/[S]/[M]/UNVERIFIED); [state whether fetch was blocked and what that means].

## 1. The short answer
One paragraph, then a numbered list of the organizing decisions, then which of them are the author's own.

## 2. The compressed statements
Thesis sentences quoted verbatim from the author's shortest treatment(s), with what the long treatment adds.

## 3. The distinctives in detail
One subsection per distinctive: text (locators), the claim, sources the author credits, what the author does that others do not, what to watch for.

## 4. Second-order emphases
Recognizable but not unique.

## 5. Interlocutor map
Table of allies and foils with counts; two or three observations.

## 6. Nearest neighbors
Comparison table; the closest ancestor and the closest contemporary.

## 7. Vulnerabilities and absences

## 8. The author's shorter statements
Chronological; verification labels; a table of "move / first built / first applied."

## 9. Reviews and reception
Journal reviews, web reviews, reception facts; what reviewers converge on; recurring criticisms; what went unremarked.

## 10. Further strategies and fetch list
What a later session with network access should open, each item one line.
```

Deliver the memo with the raw agent reports beside it, so the user can check any claim against its source lane.
